# Nightfall Cape Design Direction

## Three initial directions

### Theme Name: After-dark editorial city guide
Very dark urban fields, warm photographic light, subtle grain, thin map/grid lines, and acid-lime wayfinding signals. The emotional intent is calm curiosity: the feeling of choosing a good night without rushing.
Probability: 0.04

### Theme Name: Coastal index
A brighter, mineral, map-led guide with sea-glass tones, archival typography, and a more documentary sense of place. The emotional intent is considered local knowledge.
Probability: 0.08

### Theme Name: Signal / streetlight
A sharper utilitarian night-navigation system with clipped typography, amber signals, and route-like transitions. The emotional intent is practical confidence.
Probability: 0.03

## Chosen direction

### Design Movement
After-dark editorial digital culture: a hybrid of independent city guide, contemporary magazine, and restrained creative technology.

### Core Principles
1. Atmosphere must support discovery, never obscure it.
2. Lime is a signal color reserved for actions, section markers, arrows, active states, and key numerals.
3. Cape Town specificity should appear through coastline, mountain silhouette, neighbourhood texture, warm streetlight, and local cultural detail.
4. Every prototype claim must be labelled as fictional, measured, observed, or future-facing.

### Color Philosophy
Near-black and deep indigo create the visual space of night. Warm photographic light provides human scale. Acid lime is ownable wayfinding energy: it should feel like a sharp street sign, not general decoration. Muted violet is used only as atmospheric depth.

### Layout Paradigm
Use asymmetric editorial compositions: full-bleed image fields, offset text blocks, split event systems, index-like rails, and destination sections. Avoid a generic centered SaaS layout.

### Signature Elements
A hand-like crescent/contour mark, thin map traces and grid lines, and short mono labels that behave like location coordinates.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like finding a route: small, responsive, directional, and useful. Hover states reveal context, filters make a plan clearer, and every rich visual has a calm fallback.

### Animation
Use short transform and opacity transitions with a strong ease-out. Stagger editorial entrances lightly. Keep keyboard actions instant. Respect prefers-reduced-motion and never let motion block content.

### Typography System
Use Space Grotesk for large, tight display headlines; Georgia italic for expressive emphasis; Manrope for readable supporting copy; DM Mono for labels, metadata, and wayfinding.

### Brand Essence
A calm, visually expressive guide for people who want to find the good parts of Cape Town after sunset. Personality: curious, grounded, nocturnal.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are short and lightly poetic; CTAs are direct; microcopy is practical with a local-friend tone. Example lines: “Find your night.” “Save the useful bits; the rest can wait.”

### Wordmark & Logo
A custom lower-case wordmark paired with a crescent-contour location mark: a circular signal interrupted by a coastal notch and a small lime point.

### Signature Brand Color
Acid lime: #C4FF3D.

## Style Decisions

- Preserve the near-black field, acid-lime signal color, large soft-grotesque headlines with expressive italic emphasis, thin grid/line work, restrained navigation, and concise copy voice.
- Carry the night-city atmosphere into interior pages using the generated city image, subtle grain, map traces, warm light pools, and neighbourhood-specific content.
- Make the Discover page feel like a curated night out rather than an admin database by grouping results into editorial rows and adding context labels.
- Push Cape Town specificity through mountain/coastline visual cues, neighbourhood references, and venue details.
- Keep the lime accent disciplined as wayfinding, not general decoration.
